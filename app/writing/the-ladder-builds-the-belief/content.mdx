i wrote an essay a few weeks ago about quitting ranked ladders. this one is about a ladder i kept.

## the finding

there’s a result in maths education that i heard secondhand and then couldn’t stop reading about. success in mathematics is unusually dependent on self-efficacy: your belief, for a specific task, that you can execute it. not confidence, which is global and vague, and not mindset, whose replications have wobbled. self-efficacy is narrow and boring and measurable, and in study after study it predicts maths achievement even after you control for how good you already were. two students with the same prior ability walk into the same exam, and the one who believes the problems are doable outperforms, reliably enough that researchers have been measuring it for four decades.

that should be strange. belief isn’t knowledge. it can’t factorise anything. and yet.

## why maths, of all places

i think i know why the effect is strongest in maths, because i watch it every week. maths is the subject where you can’t half-attempt. an essay you can start badly and steer as you go; a maths problem sits there refusing to begin until you commit to an approach. and committing to an approach is exactly the move that belief governs. the students who “can’t do maths” mostly can’t start maths. they read the question, feel the blank, and the blank confirms what they already believed about themselves. panic isn’t missing knowledge. it’s a body voting no before the pencil moves.

so the first thing i actually teach a new student is rarely content. it’s the experience of starting a problem they were sure they couldn’t do, and surviving it.

## where belief comes from

bandura, who named the construct, also ranked its sources, and the ranking is the useful part. weakest: being told you’re capable. encouragement is nice and does almost nothing, which every tutor learns fast. somewhat better: watching someone like you succeed. strongest, by a distance: mastery experience. succeeding at something that genuinely resisted you. belief isn’t installed by persuasion. it’s earned by evidence, and the only admissible evidence is your own performance.

that ranking quietly redesigns the whole job of teaching. if belief is evidence, the teacher’s task is to rig the evidence stream: engineer a run of problems where the struggle is real and the success is reachable, over and over, until the student’s model of themselves updates because it has to.

## the edge, again

which is why the edge matters so much. work inside your comfort zone and nothing resisted, so nothing was proven. work too far past the edge and you gather evidence for the other side. the zone where struggle stays winnable, the one teaching people call proximal development, is where belief gets manufactured, not just where learning happens.

my learning system runs on a spacing ladder: every confusion i log comes back for a rematch after five days, then two weeks, then six, and retires as durable after three holds. i built it as a memory device, because that’s what the spacing literature says it is. but i’ve started noticing the other thing it produces. the log of everything i’ve got wrong is also, read from the other end, a log of everything i was wrong about and fixed. every held rematch is a mastery experience with a date on it. the ladder builds the schedule, and the schedule builds the belief.

## two ladders

the boulder essay ended on identity: pin yourself to a rank and your sense of self fluctuates with it, pin yourself to a capability and you just become more. self-efficacy turns out to be the psychology name for what i was groping at. a ranked ladder is a belief machine too, it’s just a badly designed one. the evidence stream is zero sum, half your data points are other people’s bad games, and the number the belief is pinned to can be taken from you in a weekend. the spacing ladder’s evidence is all mine, all dated, and all cumulative. nobody can win my rematches for me, and nobody can take a held one back.

same word, opposite machines. one ladder converts effort into a number that erodes. the other converts failure into evidence that compounds.

## the caveat

the sample size is still one and the grader is still me. the self-efficacy literature is about populations of students, not one tutor with a markdown vault, and i’d be overclaiming to say my ladder has been measured to do anything. what i can say is what it was like to reread my own log of errors and notice it had become the most reassuring document i own. every entry is something that beat me once and doesn’t anymore.

**belief is downstream of evidence. you can’t talk yourself into it, and nobody can gift it to you. but you can rig the stream, honestly, one winnable rematch at a time ■**
